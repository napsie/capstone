P0 [Normal] SENIORLINK A Rule-Based Digital Profiling and Workflow Authentication System for Senior Citizen Services in Pasig City
P3 [Normal] Abstract—SENIORLINK is a web-based system developed to support senior citizen records and service application management in Pasig City. It connects online applications, centralized profiles, electronic requirements, historical Excel records, benefit information, home-visit findings, and approved digital Senior Citizen ID records. The study used a developmental research design guided by the Rapid Application Development model, together with a quantitative descriptive evaluation. Twenty respondents, consisting of 12 Main OSCA personnel, four senior citizens, and four IT experts, evaluated 15 statements covering six software quality areas. The 300 individual ratings produced a total weighted score of 1,098 and an overall weighted mean of 3.66, interpreted as Strongly Agree. Functionality obtained the highest mean of 3.75, followed by Efficiency at 3.70 and Usability at 3.68. All six quality areas received positive assessments within the Strongly Agree interval. The findings support SENIORLINK’s potential to organize related records, support staff responsibilities, and assist application review. Continued technical evaluation and broader user participation can guide its further development.
P4 [Normal] Keywords—SENIORLINK, senior citizen services, records management, rule-based validation, application tracking, software quality evaluation
P5 [Normal] I. INTRODUCTION
P6 [Normal] Senior citizen services involve maintaining personal records, reviewing supporting documents, processing benefit requests, and coordinating activities between Main OSCA and barangay personnel. Organizing these activities within a connected platform can support access to the information needed at each stage of application review.
P7 [Normal] SENIORLINK was developed as a web-based system for senior citizen services in Pasig City. It brings together online submissions, centralized profiles, electronic documents, historical records, application tracking, benefit information, home-visit findings, and approved digital ID records. The system supports office activities while keeping final administrative decisions with authorized Main OSCA personnel.
P8 [Normal] The study addressed five research questions concerning centralized records and core modules; identity verification and request tracking; role-based and barangay-based access; rule-based checks, assistance, and home visits; and the respondents’ evaluation of the system. Its purpose was to develop a connected platform and obtain feedback from users and technical reviewers on the selected quality areas.
P9 [Normal] II. REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE
P10 [Normal] Libadia et al. [1] examined a web-based records management system for an Office of Senior Citizen Affairs. Their work provided a related basis for organizing electronic records through collection, storage, classification, and retrieval. SENIORLINK applies a similar records-management approach by linking senior citizen profiles with applications, uploaded requirements, and service history.
P11 [Normal] Anggraini et al. [2] discussed electronic document management across different industries. The organization, storage, control, and retrieval of documents are relevant to the electronic requirements handled by SENIORLINK. Connecting these requirements to the appropriate profile and application supports the review of related information.
P12 [Normal] Melendres and Aranda [3] developed and evaluated a web-based resident information management system. Their work relates to the use of centralized resident information and structured system assessment. In SENIORLINK, defined quality criteria guided feedback from Main OSCA personnel, senior citizens, and IT experts. These studies provided context for the system’s organization and evaluation approach.
P13 [Normal] III. METHODOLOGY
P14 [Normal] Research Design and System Development
P15 [Normal] The study used a developmental research design with a quantitative descriptive evaluation. Development followed the Rapid Application Development model through requirements planning, user design, construction, and cutover. These stages guided the identification of office requirements, preparation of interfaces, construction of the modules, and refinement of the application.
P16 [Normal] SENIORLINK was developed using PHP and MySQL. Its modules linked senior citizen profiles with submitted applications, uploaded documents, historical Excel records, home-visit findings, and approved digital ID records. Defined if-then conditions supported checks for missing information, invalid entries, document concerns, and possible duplicates. Staff reviewed the flagged information as part of the application process.
P17 [Normal] Participants and Evaluation Procedure
P18 [Normal] Purposive sampling was used to select 20 respondents: 12 Main OSCA personnel, four senior citizens, and four IT experts. These groups provided office, public-user, and technical perspectives on the developed system. Each respondent answered a 15-item questionnaire using a four-point rating scale.
P19 [Normal] The questionnaire covered Functionality, Reliability, Usability, Efficiency, Portability, and Maintainability. Frequency counts and weighted means summarized the 300 individual ratings. The overall weighted mean was calculated from the total weighted score divided by the total number of ratings. Means from 3.26 to 4.00 were interpreted as Strongly Agree. The reported scores describe the assessment of the participating respondents.
P20 [Normal] IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
P21 [Normal] Centralized Applications and Records
P22 [Normal] SENIORLINK connected online applications, electronic requirements, benefit requests, historical records, home-visit findings, and approved digital ID records through a centralized senior citizen profile. Authorized staff could review related information and application history within the same platform. Historical Excel import included checking and preview features that supported staff review before confirmation.
P23 [Normal] This arrangement is consistent with the electronic records-management approach discussed by Libadia et al. [1] and the document organization concepts presented by Anggraini et al. [2]. SENIORLINK applies these concepts to the records and service applications handled by Main OSCA and barangay personnel.
P24 [Normal] Identity Verification and Request Tracking
P25 [Normal] The Permanent Tracking Number remained connected to the senior citizen’s profile. After approval of the Senior Citizen ID application and assignment of an official ID number, both credentials supported access to available local benefit applications. Each submitted benefit application received a separate Transaction ID. This allowed individual requests to be identified while remaining linked to the same senior citizen profile.
P26 [Normal] Staff Access and Home Visit Coordination
P27 [Normal] Access followed the responsibilities of each role and the assigned barangay. Main OSCA personnel handled application review and final administrative decisions. SHDO personnel performed permitted barangay monitoring and assigned home-visit activities. These access boundaries supported the organization of staff responsibilities and accountability for recorded actions.
P28 [Normal] Rule-based checks identified incomplete information, invalid entries, missing requirements, and possible duplicates for review. Senior citizens could receive assistance from family members or authorized representatives when completing online applications. When a Local Pension home visit was needed, Main OSCA scheduled the visit and assigned SHDO personnel to record the findings. Main OSCA reviewed the report with the other application records. The same requirements and review process applied to applications involving home visits.

P35 [Normal] V. CONCLUSION
P36 [Normal] The study developed SENIORLINK as a centralized platform for senior citizen records and service application management. Its connected modules supported online submissions, electronic requirements, historical records, application tracking, benefit information, home-visit documentation, and approved digital ID records.
P37 [Normal] The Permanent Tracking Number and Transaction ID served distinct purposes in linking senior profiles and identifying individual applications. Staff roles and barangay assignments organized access, while rule-based checks supported application review. Assisted submissions and home-visit coordination provided additional ways for senior citizens to complete the process.
P38 [Normal] The overall weighted mean of 3.66, interpreted as Strongly Agree, reflected a positive assessment from the 20 participating respondents. The findings support SENIORLINK’s potential as a tool for organizing senior citizen services and assisting authorized personnel in their responsibilities.
P39 [Normal] VI. RECOMMENDATIONS
P40 [Normal] The researchers recommend continued refinement and technical evaluation of SENIORLINK. Backup restoration exercises, deployment checks, and testing after system changes can strengthen reliability, portability, and maintainability. Regular review of user feedback can also guide improvements to navigation, instructions, and accessibility.
P41 [Normal] Main OSCA should review staff permissions and barangay assignments whenever responsibilities change. Validation rules should remain aligned with current office requirements and procedures. Clear coordination between Main OSCA and SHDO personnel should be maintained for home-visit scheduling, documentation, and review.
P42 [Normal] Further evaluation should include SHDO personnel, authorized representatives, additional senior citizens, and more barangays. Future researchers may measure record retrieval time, application processing time, historical-record import accuracy, and performance with larger datasets. These measurements can complement the questionnaire findings and guide the continued development of the system.
