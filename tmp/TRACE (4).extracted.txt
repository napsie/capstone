P136 [Normal] ABSTRACT
P139 [Normal] SENIORLINK is a web-based system developed to help the Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) and Senior Help Desk Officers (SHDO) manage senior citizen records and service applications in Pasig City. It brings together senior citizen profiles, online applications, uploaded requirements, historical records, benefit information, home-visit findings, and temporary digital Senior Citizen IDs in one platform. Senior citizens or their representatives can submit applications online and monitor their requests using a permanent Tracking Number. After their Senior Citizen ID application is approved and an official ID number is assigned, they can use these credentials to access available benefit applications. Each submitted request has its own Benefit Tracking number ID while
P140 [Normal] remaining connected to the senior citizen’s profile. Main OSCA personnel review applications and manage citywide records, while SHDO personnel access records and perform permitted tasks within their assigned barangay. The system uses predefined rules to check required information, age requirements, document submissions, and possible duplicate records. These checks support staff review, while authorized personnel remain responsible for administrative decisions. Other features include historical Excel record import, Local Pension home-visit scheduling and evaluation, announcements, PDF and Excel reports, and encrypted backups. The project followed the Rapid Application Development model and was evaluated by 20 respondents consisting of 12 Main OSCA personnel, four senior citizens, and four IT experts. A 15-item questionnaire covered Functionality, Reliability, Usability, Efficiency, Portability, and Maintainability. The system received an overall weighted mean of 3.66, interpreted as Strongly Agree. These ratings indicate a positive assessment from the participants, while further testing is needed before broader implementation.

INTRODUCTION
P149 [Heading 2] The Problem and its Background
P150 [Normal] The rise in the numbers of people aged 65 years and older is being observed in various parts of the globe. The report published by the United Nations Population Fund (2024) states that people aged 65 years and older accounted for 5.5% of the global population in 1974 and 10.3% in 2024. The 2020 Census of Population and Housing shows that the Philippine population has approximately 9.22 million people, who are 60 years old and above, meaning that senior citizens represent roughly 8.5% of the total population of the country (Philippine Statistics Authority, 2022). As the population of senior citizens increases, the necessity of having accurate database records and transparent procedures associated with the delivery of effective services increases as well. The government has taken initiatives to start the use of digitization for identification and service provision. An example of the usage of digital technology is presented by the launch of the digital National Senior Citizens ID initiated by the National Commission of Senior Citizens (NCSC) and the Department of Information and Communication Technology through the eGovPH Application. The issued digital ID allows senior citizens to have a device needed to show an official ID.This effort indicates how digital identification can facilitate verification. However, it does not deal with the independent processes and benefits of Pasig City. According to David et al. (2023), local government technology is effective only if all aspects are taken into consideration: personnel, processes, and technological tools. Therefore, Pasig
P151 [Normal] City needs a system that adheres to the functions and job practices of its agencies. The Office for Senior Citizens Affairs (OSCA) of Pasig City oversees local services for senior citizens. In this paper, Main OSCA stands for the head office, while Main OSCA personnel stands for its employees. Senior Help Desk Officers (who are also referred to as SHDO personnel) carry out the different authorized services belonging to the barangay in question. In this case, senior citizens are the applicants and beneficiaries of these services. The term authorized representatives, in this respect, is utilized to refer to individuals who are allowed to help or represent a senior citizen. The services provided are limited to applying for Senior Citizen IDs and using Land Bank, Enrollment, Local Senior Pension, Milestone Cash Gift, and Burial Assistance.
P152 [Normal] Managing and locating paper records can be complicated especially when similar records are stored in various places or with different staff members. A record could be missing, damaged, double-entered, or lagging while the personnel look for it. In their research Anggraini et al. (2024) highlight that electronic systems of document management make possible the safe keeping, management, and retrieval of records, which implies a lower reliance on paper. Moreover, the authors of the research Libadia et al. (2025) demonstrate an application of a centralized web solution for gaining access and retrieving senior person information much quicker. The digital process allows minimizing unnecessary visits to the office for senior people with disabilities for whom it may be hard to get there.
P153 [Normal] SENIORLINK is an internet-based centralized program created to have profiles of senior citizens, online requests, and resident benefits records in Pasig City. The system links uploaded requirements and progress tracking, home visit report, and issuance date with required documents. Public applications are added to the priority of Main OSCA for approval. The officials of Main OSCA verify the information received, while SHDO workers verify barangay records and submit the results for home visit. Workflow authentication is defined as confirming the user’s rights, the rights of the staff and updates in the application status.
P155 [Heading 2] Review of Related Literature
P156 [Normal] The literature is categorized into three categories, namely; digital records and profiles of elderly persons; document and process management and use of rules to support workflow. Each category states the findings of the previous studies that influenced this work as well as the unique characteristics of local workflows fulfilled by this project.
P158 [Normal] Digital Profiling Frameworks and Senior Citizen Welfare Management
P159 [Normal] The local government's success in meeting the needs of the elderly depends on accurate data for planning and service delivery. Ordinance No. 40 Series of 2021 in Pasig City examines local policies regarding the programs that promote the interests of elderly citizens (Pasig City Government, 2021).There is much more to fulfilling those obligations than simply having a list of names. Employees need to be aware of factors such as verifying identity, age, address, application history, and some other local services related to that record. In case information is scattered among several folders or files, the person responsible can waste a lot of time checking the data.
P160 [Normal] Digital identification is illustrated through the case of the electronic National Senior Citizens ID. The National Commission of Senior Citizens (2025) introduced the ID through the eGovPH App together with the Department of Information and Communications Technology. The individuals who have been registered may use digital IDs for receiving discounts, transit, and various other services. The purpose of this program is to free senior citizens from depending on a physical card, thus offering an additional way to verify their identity. The project is nationwide while SENIORLINK is oriented towards local authorities. Moreover, there are certain instruments that the local branches of each organization will require. Libadia et al. (2025) developed an internet-based scheme for the electronic records management of the Office of Senior Citizen Affairs, in the Philippines. The scheme united the stages of collecting, storing, classifying, and retrieving senior citizen records within one system, which granted access only to authorized personnel. This experience can be useful for SENIORLINK, since the leading OSCA has to look through full electronic documents without waiting for the printed versions from local offices.
P161 [Normal] According to the developers Melendres and Aranda (2024), a software system that helped manage the resident information was created which made record keeping easier. The evaluation verified that the developed software met quality criteria as far as maintaining records and providing barangay services was concerned. However, even if the present analysis is related to general resident information and not specifically to senior citizens’ benefits, the significance of the common source of necessary information is noticeable.
P162 [Normal] The barangay office automation project of dela Cerna (2023) implemented resident profiling, transaction processing, report generation, and information access for staff members. Although the project was not intended for senior citizens’ benefits, its use of distinct functions is applicable to SENIORLINK. Meanwhile, OSCA personnel are responsible for the monitoring provision and final decisions and SHDO personnel are only in charge of carrying out monitoring tasks in barangay areas. The utilization of an authorized representative does not affect the review process.
P164 [Normal] Integration and Identified Gaps in Digital Profiling
P165 [Normal] The materials show various aspects of e-public service. In particular, the City Ordinance of Pasig provides a legal basis of senior citizen programs at the local level. Moreover, the National Citizens’ Service Center initiative presents a case where a digital ID can be utilized for identification purposes at the national level. According to Libadia et al. (2025), OSCA records were examined, according to Melendres and Aranda (2024), the focus was on resident records, and dela Cerna (2023) studied the process of barangay office automation. In general, these research back up the idea of centralized record-keeping and restricted access, however, none of them represents the entire local operation model suggested for the SENIORLINK.
P166 [Normal] The studies that were analyzed provide minimal information regarding how to arrange home visits along with applications from senior citizens. SENIORLINK integrates home visit scheduling, barangay discoveries, local digital ID retrieval, and disbursing funds with the Main OSCA process of giving information. The system makes it possible to cater to applicants with limited movement but without giving preferences to those who have assistance in applying.
P167 [Normal] The designed response relies on equal review and separation of work. Authorized representatives may help an applicant in the same way as others. If there is a need for a home visit, the Main OSCA will schedule the visit while the assigned SHDO will make a report. After this, the Main OSCA will review the report and make its decision. This method is designed to help people with immobility while keeping the approval process honest for all applicants in Pasig City.
P169 [Normal] Business Process Management and Electronic Document Management in Public Administration
P170 [Normal] No improvement in public service could happen if the only innovation implemented is a digital profile. According to Plattfaut and Grisold (2025), business process management is a continuous aspect of digital innovations. Their research is not restricted to local governments, but it represents a valuable idea in this case since it implies revisions of the procedures when the organization changes the technology and routines. SENIORLINK needs to understand the procedure from a submission to a review, an assessment in the field, when necessary, implementation of final actions, and digital ID creation with benefit scheduling.
P171 [Normal] By combining related documents, electronic document management makes the process easier. According to Anggraini et al. (2024) electronic document management systems are meant to manage documents, including their organization, storage, control, as well as retrieval (Anggraini et al., 2024) . The article also lists some of the issues typical for physical documents, such as destruction, loss, and difficulties in locating proper records. Libadia et al. (2025) successfully utilize the idea by building a records management system based on the OSCA concept. The organization of SENIORLINK is in line with this, as all the documents, fees, home visits assessment, and any changes in status are linked back to one electronic document.
P172 [Normal] Digital adoption depends on employee skills, processes, technical skills, security, and privacy among other factors. David et al. (2023) group these issues according to the relationship between people, processes, and technology. Their study warns that the use of the new instrument may fail if it does not correspond with the company’s practice or users’ knowledge about the responsibilities they have. In SENIORLINK, the status should be valuable only if it is updated by the person who is assigned to it. Hence, training, limitations, and accountability are regarded as inseparable components of the process rather than independent issues.
P173 [Normal] The ideas support an electronic workflow system with fixed user level. The Central OSCA accepts and assesses online applications, makes scheduling of field works, takes decisions, assigns and issues local electronic IDs, and draws up local grant disbursement plans. SHDO staff may assist the elderly in filling in the online public application form, but in the end, the application form is to be sent directly to the Central OSCA. The SHDO employees have the right to monitor the applications that have come in from the barangays, perform house visits, produce evaluation reports and aid in the disbursement of assistance. They may neither withdraw applications nor grant benefits to individuals, nor anything to influence or modify the application.
P175 [Normal] Consolidation of Process and Document Management Frameworks
P176 [Normal] The studies of both the process and the documents lead to the conclusion that converting a paper document into an electronic document is not sufficient. Employees should know who to send the document to and check that he/she received it correctly and that the document moves to the next part of the process. Plattfaut and Grisold (2025) analyze the issue from the process perspective, while Anggraini et al. (2024) focus on the issue from the perspective of document management. David et al. (2023) explain how both mentioned perspectives apply to the local government service staff.
P177 [Normal] With the help of online applications, household visits, local digital ID viewing, and scheduling of releases, SENIORLINK integrates various processes into one comprehensive system. OSCA has the final say in terms of approvals and reviews. SHDO staff can only support the public in filling up forms and ensure that records about the barangays are kept updated. However, they are not in charge of processing applications at their office.
P179 [Normal] Rule-Based Logic Systems and Workflow Automation in Social Welfare
P180 [Normal] Electronic records, however, require humans to perform many everyday tasks. A form must be assigned, further updates must be made, or a reminder must be delivered to a reviewer. The workflow software as described in NDL Software Limited (2024) is able to process information in several stages, apply specified conditions, send notifications, and execute scheduled operations. This document is not an independent research paper but serves as a vendor service proposal, thus it is only meant to demonstrate the functionalities of existing workflow solutions. Similarly, in SENIORLINK, the system is capable of routing applications to the main OSCA queue and sending notifications about home visit appointments.
P181 [Normal] The workflow might implement rule-based logic to determine what step to take next. Research by Papadopoulos et al. (2022) involved rules and rule engines in clinical decision support systems. They suggest that the knowledge of experts can be put in writing in the form of rules and used for comparison with the data for a particular case. Although their area of focus is healthcare instead of welfare administration, the idea can still be used in record checking. A rule can determine whether the required field is empty, whether the date is invalid, whether the file is absent, or whether the profile matches with another one already present in the database. Based on these results, it can be directed to a human worker.
P182 [Normal] Although there are some differences between the traditional rule-based model and an AI model, they both have their legal implications as discussed by Enqvist (2024). While established laws and rules are in common language and can be easily interpreted by practitioners, it can be problematic to explain the learned model's working mechanism as it is inherently complicated with plenty of variables and hidden weights. There is a point in how a fixed rule does not guarantee fairness. Wrong rules will make the same mistake until they get changed. This means that all rules must come from a standard practice and get reviewed whenever the rules of the game change.
P183 [Normal] SENIORLINK's criteria are limited only to validation and workflow support. Rules can indicate missing data, a possible duplicate, an incorrect entry, or an entry that requires further proof. A rule can also mean that an application is ready for review by a human being. Rules however do not determine a person’s entitlement and do not automatically approve, reject, or rank an applicant. Cases presenting via a representative and those with home visits are subject to normal review rules.
P184 [Normal] Human accountability is essential when technology is employed in a decision-making process. The opinion of Busuioc (2021) is that the results of algorithms in the process of public administration should be subject to scrutiny and provide the possibility of human intervention in the process. The accountability is based on the agent using the technology. Hence, SENIORLINK has to capture the rule referenced in the warning, the person reviewing the warning, and the follow-up action taken. The senior OSCA representative has either to change the entry in the register or ask additional questions instead of blindly trusting the machines.
P185 [Normal] It is essential to test the workflow and the rules with the people who will be using it. Melendres and Aranda (2024) created their resident information system using the Rapid Application Development method, which included applying some specific ISO 25010 quality software criteria to assess the developed program. The works of the authors prove the importance of carrying out the testing of obtained outcomes to make sure that it meets users’ needs. SENIORLINK will be able to test full and non-full applications, cases of home visits, cases with possible duplicates, and changes in the status of the cases carried out by the Main OSCA staff and SHDO staff. This combined testing helps to understand whether the proposed rule is implemented correctly and legitimately before it becomes widely applied.
P187 [Normal] Analytical Alignment with System Decision Logic
P188 [Normal] There are a number of studies on automation made from different perspectives. For example, in NDL Software Limited (2024) the authors explain the operation of a given business process management system. The research conducted by Papadopoulos et al. (2022) analyses how the rule engines are being implemented in health systems. Enqvist (2024) speaks about the legal problems connected to the legislation of benefits distribution, while Busuioc (2021) has researched the problems of accountability in the public sector. The technical writers demonstrate the information streams through the established phases, but legal and public administration researchers challenge the possibility of deciphering the results of the automated output. This difference allows having a clearer demarcation line for SENIORLINK. While speed is critical in verification, it should not be the only objective of an application for social purposes. A rule could produce the same output for the same data but still be incorrect if the data or the rule is incorrect. Therefore, SENIORLINK should use rules to highlight a record instead of determining the individual qualified for a benefit. The employee should be able to understand the rationale for the alert and compare it to the formal requirements. These proposed regulations can also help the authorities to check if the applicant is old enough, if the fields required have been filled correctly, if the document dates given are recent or not, and if there are chances that some similar application is already there or has already been applied before. This way, the verification of local requirements becomes easy, and so do the related rules in accordance with Pasig City Ordinance No. 40, Series of 2021 (Pasig City Government, 2021).The authorized personnel of Main OSCA will examine the residency information and other documents instead of the automatic verification done by the system. The rules won't assign benefits or approve the application or make any hardships for eligibility.
P189 [Normal] The electronic record of activities correlates the changes of state and findings from house visits with the actions of the staff being accountable for them. The history assists the Main OSCA officials with their internal supervision. However, it cannot prove compliance with all outside audit requirements.
P191 [Normal] Theoretical and Operational Justification of the Study
P192 [Normal] The literature that has been reviewed points to three segments of the framework. The findings of studies concerning digital profiles and records management demonstrate the need for senior citizens’ data to be stored in one location and under control (Libadia et al., 2025; Melendres & Aranda, 2024). The evidence produced by researchers on process and document management shows how electronic records are used in the case where the office stream and works are also defined (Anggraini et al., 2024; Plattfaut & Grisold, 2025). The research regarding rule engines as well as public accountability allows understanding how technology can be used in the process of providing people with ethical decisions (Busuioc, 2021; Enqvist, 2024; Papadopoulos et al., 2022).
P193 [Normal] Moreover, the literature contains an operational void. The examined systems do not feature one local senior citizen platform that brings together an OSCA review queue, barangay monitoring, scheduled home visits, local digital ID viewing, and benefit release scheduling. They pay little attention to applicants who find it hard to get to an office, and they do not demonstrate how simple rules can help in the review without resulting in automatic approval or preference. Therefore, the problem is one of coordination and accessibility, not an indication that all the currently existing systems are inaccessible to bedridden senior citizens.
P194 [Normal] SENIORLINK utilizes centralized profile, entirely digital application process, managed import of Excel records, and limited rule-driven verification. Main OSCA performs import of records, review of applications, scheduling, decision-making, and creating local digital identity. After obtaining approval, a senior can apply for other benefits using the permanent tracking number and the Senior Citizen's ID that was issued to him/her. Each application is marked with a separate benefit tracking number. The SHDO staff may assist senior citizens with filling in the online form, follow the applications within the assigned barangay, visit clients, send evaluations, etc. The system logs any necessary actions but leaves the final decision to the Main OSCA authorized agents.

P202 [Normal (Web)] To Researchers and Future Researchers: The study may serve as a reference for future projects involving digital records, workflow management, and rule-based checks in local government. It provides an example of how software can support public employees without replacing their authority. Future researchers may build on the system by studying accessibility, privacy, security, records management, or other local government services. Any later application of the approach should still be adjusted to the law, office procedures, and needs of its intended users
P203 [Normal] Statement of the Problem
P204 [Normal] The personnel of OSCA operate with senior citizen records and evaluate local applications in Pasig City. SHDO personnel support barangay monitoring projects and conduct assigned home visits. Keeping these operations connected is hard because of separated files and manual recordings. The processes of requirements search have shown repeated entries of same data, uncompleted papers, sluggish tracing of records, and issues with the standing of applications. In addition, the historical records must pass through a specially designed import process before being put into the system.
P205 [Normal] For senior citizens who are bedridden, unwell, or have difficulty traveling, this process can be problematic. A family member may help with filing, but the applicant still has to fulfil the same requirements and go through the same step as others. In cases where a home visit is required, Main OSCA has to set up the visit and send the application to the appropriate SHDO. After that the SHDO also has to submit findings so that Main OSCA can consider all data and make a decision.
P206 [Normal] To bring together both the documentation and the processes, the research team has developed the SENIORLINK service. The project examined applications and records, verified information about people, verified personnel, confirmed adherence policy, and tested usability of supported applications and home visits. After that, SENIORLINK service was tested through the standardized questionnaire.
P207 [Normal] Specifically, the study seeks to answer the following questions:
P208 [Normal (Web)] How can SENIORLINK manage online applications, centralized senior citizen profiles, electronic requirements, historical Excel records, home-visit results, benefit schedules, classification and approved digital Senior Citizen IDs?
P209 [Normal (Web)] How can the system securely allow an approved senior citizen to apply for different available local benefits using the permanent tracking number and issued Senior Citizen ID while generating a separate benefits tracking number for each submitted application?
P210 [List Paragraph] How can role-based and barangay-based access allow Main OSCA personnel to review and decide on applications, Main OSCA administrators to manage accounts across barangays, and authorized SHDO personnel to view approved digital IDs only within their assigned barangay while carrying out permitted monitoring and home-visit tasks?
P211 [Normal (Web)] How can SENIORLINK support accurate and accessible application processing through rule-based validation of incomplete information, invalid entries, missing requirements, and possible duplicate records, while providing online assistance and scheduled home visits for bedridden, ill, or mobility-impaired senior citizens without granting special application priority or replacing the final eligibility decision of authorized OSCA personnel?
P212 [List Paragraph] What ratings do Main OSCA personnel, senior citizens, and information technology (IT) experts give SENIORLINK in terms of Functionality, Reliability, Usability, Efficiency, Portability, and Maintainability?
P215 [Normal] Scope and Limitation of the Study
P216 [Normal] The research involves the designing, improving, and assessment of SENIORLINK: A Rule-Based Digital Profiling and Workflow Authentication System for the Services of Senior Citizens within the boundaries of Pasig City. The system is designed to assist in the internal handling of the local profiles of senior citizens along with the applications. The scope indicates the capabilities that are imputed in the system while the limitations highlight the services that are not part of the project.
P217 [Normal] Scope of the Study
P218 [Normal (Web)] The developed system includes the following core modules and features:
P219 [Normal (Web)] Rule-Based Application Checking: This module utilizes basic if-then rules to conduct checks on the applicant's age, completeness of required information, dates of document submissions, and any other local rules applicable. It will not approve or deny an application automatically. Any warning generated by the system still needs to go through the verification of the authorized Main OSCA staff. Validation of the Pasig residency is not included in the automated rules.
P220 [Normal (Web)] Account and Role Management: The main community development officers are authorized to set up administrator and SHDO accounts in different barangays. The public portal is used by everyone, including senior citizens and representatives. SHDO accounts cannot be utilized by senior citizens for their applications, decisions or changing of assignment.
P221 [Normal (Web)] There are a few requirements for submitting forms for senior citizens and their representatives via the public portal: The SHDO team may help with the public form submissions but will not use their staff accounts for submitting the application. The system stores all submitted documentation and application history. All requests to produce original documentation will be processed according to accepted office procedures.
P222 [Normal (Web)] Permanent Senior Access Tracking Number  and Application Benefits Tracking Number:  Following the completion of the initial online application, the system shall issue the permanent Tracking Number to the senior citizen account. It is mandatory that the permanent Tracking Number is utilized to monitor the registration. Right after the Main OSCA approves the registration and registers the local Senior Citizen ID number, the senior citizen is obliged to present both Tracking Number while applying for any other local benefits. Every application for benefits guarantees the acquisition of another tracking number for monitoring of the process of each individual case.
P223 [Normal (Web)] Historical Import of Records in Excel: The allowed personnel of OSCA can bring in the prior senior citizen records on certified Excel files. Before the actions of the system where the records will be inserted, it checks the file format, necessary columns, missing data, and possible duplicate users. The user will see a preview and validation of imported records then confirm everything.
P224 [Normal (Web)] Digital Management of Senior Citizens' IDs: The authentication of the applicant is carried out, and only then the respective personnel of the OSCA create a local digital ID of the senior citizens, who can check it after passing the authentication. The only time a representative may access it is if Main OSCA has recorded a valid authorization. SHDO personnel may see the approved digital IDs of senior citizens residing in their respective barangays, but they do not have the power to generate, modify, or distribute the IDs.
P225 [Normal (Web)] Home-Visit Monitoring: A home visit may be scheduled by the Main OSCA if the applicant is not able to visit the assessment center or if field evaluation is necessary for the chosen service. In the existing Local Senior Pension procedure, an accomplished visit is required before verification. The visit is subsequently recorded by the designated agency personnel and checked by the OSCA prior to a decision. Home visit does not make the application a priority.
P226 [Normal (Web)] Local Benefit Release Scheduling:  Authorized personnel of Main OSCA will be in charge of deciding the date, time as well as place of the Local benefit release. SHDO personnel will be the ones accessing the schedule and will be called upon at the venue chosen. Moreover, the system will document the release; nonetheless, it will not be used for money transfer or release.
P227 [Normal (Web)] There are limitations in accessing the access of SHDO staff: The members of SHDO staff will assist other people with their applications using the public portal, monitor them, see the approved digital IDs in their area, complete the related evaluations at home, and check the schedules for the processing of their applications. They cannot submit applications for old persons using their service account.
P228 [Normal (Web)] The electronic activity records:   important acts performed such as submitting applications, reviewing documents, changing statuses, warning about regulations, conducting home visits, creating and viewing digital IDs, and scheduling releases among others. This record helps in the monitoring of activities internally as well as subsequently viewed by approved personnel assigned.
P229 [Normal (Web)] Limitations of the Study
P230 [Normal] To maintain technical feasibility and comply with existing local policies, the system is bound by the following limitations:
P231 [Normal (Web)] Lack of Integration with Other Systems: SENIORLINK functions as a distinct local government system. There will be no integrative link with the Social Security System, Government Service Insurance System, eGovPH App, any bank, and other external information systems.
P232 [Normal (Web)] Prohibition of Data Sharing with National Agency: SENIORLINK does not transmit any information to the Department of Social Welfare and Development, National Commission of Senior Citizens or any other government agency. All information that is recorded through SENIORLINK will be incorporated in authorized local service providers.
P233 [Normal (Web)] Applies to Pasig City Services Only: SENIORLINK will only cover senior citizens' records, applications, and local benefits provided by Pasig City. It will not process matters related to social pensions, cash assistance or any benefits provided by the national government.
P234 [Normal (Web)] No Direct Money Disbursement: The system will not operate any funds. It will also not support wire transfers, use of cards, credit card payments, or electronic wallet payments. SENIORLINK will assist only in storing valid local advantage information along with a release schedule and venue arranged by administrator.
P235 [Normal (Web)] Final Decisions and Review by Humans: The system can halt a submission that is incomplete or an invalid alteration of the workflow and can identify a suspected duplicate. However, these controls are separate from the final approval or rejection, which is in the hands of the qualified Main OSCA personnel.
P236 [Normal (Web)] Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning are not deployed: SENIORLINK does not plan to place applicants in a ranking order, predict eligibility or provide recommendations regarding who is entitled to benefits. Only elementary, easy-to-comprehend if-then rules will be utilized for undertaking basic checks of the applicant process.
P237 [Normal (Web)] Automated Verification of Residency in Pasig Not Available: The system fails to provide automatic verification of the applicant's duration of residence in Pasig City using any other government databases. The residency information and accompanying documentation must be examined by authorized officials of the Main OSCA.
P238 [Normal (Web)] There is no Benefit Tracking Number-based Priority or Agency: The original Tracking Number will serve to trace the initial registration. When approved, it can also be used together with the local Senior Citizen ID to verify the senior citizen in other applications for local service. Each subsequent application for benefits will have its own application tracking number. Tracking Number have neither priority nor proof of agency nor access to any senior citizens’ records.
P239 [Normal (Web)] No preference is given to applications for home visits: Home visits might be arranged for people that may not otherwise be able to get to the relevant application office. However, the visit will not allow the application to be completed any sooner than others or affect the person that will ultimately be making the decision about the application.
P240 [Normal (Web)] Restrained Functions of Barangay or SHDO: The staff of SHDO can guide a candidate in filling the public online form; however, the candidate submits the application via the public portal to Main OSCA. They are able to oversee applications and approved digital IDs within their assigned barangay, carry out assigned home visitations, and report the findings. But they cannot accept or reject applications, rearranging the queue, import previous data, produce digital IDs, or see the records of another barangay.
P241 [Normal (Web)] Local Digital ID only: SENIORLINK shows a local digital Senior Citizen ID record once the necessary approval is given. The research does not prove that this display acts as a substitute for the physical OSCA card or the national digital ID. The system does not interface with the eGovPH App.
P242 [Normal (Web)] Electronic Submission:   The system allows for electronic forms and uploaded requirements but does not facilitate the transfer of printed folders from one office to another. There remains a need to use the original document in accordance with the approved office rules.
P243 [Normal (Web)] The Electronic Audit History: Indicates the system logs application changes, user events, rule warnings delivered by the system, result of home visits, and any planned releases. The history will be used for internal control and would allow for future review; however, the study does not state that it meets any of the standards required by the Commission on Audit or other auditing entities.

P268 [Normal] Three-Tier Architectural Framework of SENIORLINK
P270 [Normal] SENIORLINK uses three layers: presentation, application, and data. The presentation layer contains the web pages, the application layer processes requests and applies rules, and the data layer stores records and documents. Keeping these duties separate supports maintenance and access control.
P271 [Normal] Senior citizens, authorized representatives, Main OSCA personnel, and SHDO personnel use the web interfaces. The central application handles permitted requests and controls access to records and uploaded documents. Users do not connect directly to the database.
P274 [Normal] Detailed Structural Layer Description
P275 [Normal] Presentation Layer
P276 [Normal] The Presentation Layer encompasses the sites and forms that facilitate the interaction of users with SENIORLINK. It takes user input and displays the information obtained from the Application Layer. This layer is not responsible for the creation, modification, and deletion of information in the database. The Public Online Portal is accessible to seniors and people who help seniors. It includes a form for registration, benefit applications, documents submission, status tracking of applications and registrar forms, identity verification interfaces, and the secured view of the local digital Senior Citizen ID.
P277 [Normal] The Main OSCA portal has functions such as evaluating applications, checking documents, processing user profiles, importing, scheduling, decision-making, generating digital IDs, and producing reports. The department administrator account on the portal is referred to as Department Admin. The term Main OSCA administrator here refers to the employee of Main OSCA who is authorized to perform account management and administrative functions. This means that Main OSCA administrator is an employment status in Main OSCA staff and not the name of a specific respondent group.
P278 [Normal] The SHDO Portal can only be utilized by authorized officials. This means that the portal contains the filed applications and approved digital Senior Citizen ID cards pertaining only to the officer’s barangay location. The information that is included in the applicant's details is restricted to only that which may be necessary for the officer's monitoring purposes or task assignment. The SHDO officers can input findings from home visits and check approved release schedules. However, they do not retain the power of final decisions-making, developing or changing digital IDs, making changes regarding the queue, importing records from the past, activating permanent IDs, or obtaining data regarding another barangay.
P280 [Normal] Application Layer
P281 [Normal] The application layer is responsible for processing logic. The PHP application receives requests from browsers, authenticates the user, checks the user permissions, processes the request and forwards approved requests to the data layer.
P282 [Normal] The Authentication and Role-Based Access Module regulates employee login, account status, session management, and permissions. It classifies Main OSCA administrators, authorized Main OSCA personnel, SHDO personnel, and users of the public portal. Each request is processed according to the user's role, and in case of SHDO personnel, the barangay related to the account. The same restrictions will apply to the process of creating accounts: Main OSCA administrators will be able to create accounts for any barangay.
P283 [Normal] The Senior Citizen Profile and Application Management module takes care of initial registrations, local benefit applications, profile updates, electronic requirements, and application history. It links each senior citizen who is eligible to one central profile while keeping separate records for every application. The Rule-Based Validation module checks the minimum age requirement and other requirements like if all required fields are filled and required electronic documents are submitted with the help of fixed if-then statements. The module will alert the main OSCA body to check the applications; however, it will not approve or reject any application automatically.
P284 [Normal] The workflow functions manage the various stages of a process - submission, review, amendments, necessary home visits, making a final decision, and planning for the release. The system notifies the users of the current status and previous activities. SHDO staff submit the findings of their visits to the authorized representatives of Main OSCA who take the final decision about the case.
P285 [Normal] The Credential and Tracking Module operates with credentials throughout the course of the process. The registration process starts with obtaining a permanent profile tracking number. The tracking number allows the users to track the registration process before it is approved. After obtaining confirmation of the documents and identity verification from Main OSCA, the tracking number can be used for obtaining the Senior Citizen ID and for further applications. The applications receive special tracking numbers.
P286 [Normal] The historical record import process allows authorized Excel or CSV documents. It verifies the format and all necessary information using its pre-import preview. Main OSCA personnel confirm the origin of the records and address the problems identified. It is worth noting that by simply processing one row it does not mean that all details are accurately specified.
P287 [Normal] The Home-Visit Management Module ensures that Main OSCA personnel assign and schedule a home visit to the suitable SHDO. The appointed person has access to all required details for the home visit and submits the evaluation results. Main OSCA observes the evaluation results along with the other details of the application prior to reaching the final decision.
P288 [Normal] The Local Digital ID and Benefit Scheduling Module guarantees the creation and safe access to the local digital ID of Senior Citizen after approval of the registration. Additionally, the module enables the personnel of Main OSCA to memo the day, period of time, and place where the local benefit will be released.
P289 [Normal] The Reporting Module produces various reports that are required by the Main OSCA for the purpose of monitoring and providing decisions. These reports include registration and applicant totals, the status of applications, status of approved and declined applications, possible duplicates, historical record imports, the planning and implementation of in-home visits, interval of local benefits release, and employee actions recorded. Access to the reports will depend on the position of the user and authorization he/she has.
P290 [Normal] The Electronic Activity Logging Module logs important events of the system, which include the attempts of logging on, submission of application, documents review, notifications about the rules, changes in the status, import of the historical records, operations of verifying identity, the conclusion of home visit, final decision reaching, production of digital ID, Tracking Number replacement, and scheduling of benefits.
P291 [Normal] Data Layer
P292 [Normal] The data layer collects and organizes information of seniors, offices and establishments, applications, and documents through a common database management system, whereas the documents are stored in storage controlled by the server. The application layer ensures access to the data.
P293 [Normal] The Central Database keeps records of senior citizens' profiles, employees' accounts, roles, registrations, applications for local benefits, statuses of applications, warnings, home-visit schedules and findings, import of historical records, local digital ID data, schedules of benefits payments, records of  tracking numbers and reporting.
P294 [Normal] The electronic files are kept in a safe, secure and monitored area of the server. The database maintains the relevant information about the files like their name, type, reference for an application, date of uploading and authorizing person. Before accepting the files, the system checks both the kind of file allowed and all other accepted upload parameters.
P295 [Normal] The activity reports show the action performed, user who was performing it, record affected and the time of the action done. Access is provided in accordance with permissions of staff members. This allows possible audit of the performed action.
P298 [Normal] Inter-Layer Communication
P299 [Normal] The connection process starts when the web browser submits the request to the given PHP application. This application examines the session, the role of the user, and the information providing access. The implementation of HTTPS is mandatory to safeguard the transmitted information.
P300 [Normal] When the time comes to retrieve or store the data, the PHP application starts the communication with the Data Layer through the controlled connection to the database. The PDO and prepared statements are employed during the database operations in order to minimize the risks related to unsafe queries. The Application Layer sends back the validated result to the Presentation Layer either as a rendered page or as the information needed for the interface update.
P301 [Normal] The Presentation Layer is not connecting directly with the database. All access profile activities, document processing, change in status, rule checking, report request, and other operations will be performed through the Application Layer. This policy enables uniform application of validation and access processes to each request.
P303 [Normal] Operational Boundaries and Technical Constraints
P304 [Normal] SENIORLINK acts as a separate local system. There’s no direct link between it and the national agency databases, eGovPH, banks, or e-wallets.
P305 [Normal] The only personnel from Main OSCA with the right to carry out this task are allowed to import historical data in Excel or CSV formats. The preview of the validation identifies the records that need correction.
P306 [Normal] The final responsibility for the correctness of information belongs to Main OSCA employees. The evidence of residency is checked by Main OSCA employees and not verified through a third-party database.
P307 [Normal] The presence of validation messages does not annul the administrative decision. Thus, the system is able to store information about local benefits and their timetable.
P308 [Normal] Actual payment release is not done through the system. The system architecture implements varying safety measures, including access control, checking of session activity, information validation, usage of prepared statements in the databases, and controlled document access. In such a way, the data is retained.
P310 [Normal] Algorithm Discussion
P311 [Normal] SENIORLINK has fixed validation procedures. General functions check on some parameters, while the chosen service decides which extra fields and requirements must be considered. Input and settings always produce the same output. Machine learning is not used by the system to make decisions about who should receive a certain benefit.
P312 [Normal] Common Validation Steps
P313 [Normal] The entire operation verifies the intended action and necessary or authorized staff credentials or permissions. In addition, the long-term tracking number and senior citizen identification must correspond to the permitted record. The next step is to check the relevant facts and documents and issue changes or caution notices. The existence of the documents does not confirm their authenticity. The officers of OSCA evaluate what's inside the documents and make ultimate determinations.
P315 [Normal] Age and Service Requirements
P316 [Normal] Age is determined through the calculation of the date of birth and the date of reference. To establish the age of someone, the year of birth is deducted from the year of the reference and if the individual has not celebrated a birthday as of yet then deducted by one more year. For example, a person born on October 15, 1961, is already 64 years old on October 1, 2026, but turns 65 on October 15.
P317 [Normal] As for the application for senior citizen ID, certain personal information, documents and age checks have to be submitted. Each type of application has a different list of requirements which may vary from issuing of an ID to replacement, transfer and any information changes. The evidence of residence will be reviewed by the staff of the main OSCA unit. The system will not verify if the residence is true or not, by using an outside database. In case of the Land Bank Cash Card Enrollment process, the information regarding enrollment and identification will be checked. It will, however, allow you to use only a 23-character name. For example, a name with 24 characters will be rejected.
P318 [Normal] The Local Senior Pension applies the configured minimum age of 65, and gathers income, family, housing, and health data. A mandatory home visit has to be made prior to performing last verification. Findings are recorded by the SHDO personnel, the application is passed to the Main OSCA staff.
P319 [Normal] Milestone Cash Gift obtains date of birth and configured brackets (80, 85, 90, 95, and 100) for the review of claimant data and evidence. The position instead of the use of the pre-configured brackets must check the adherence to the procedures of approval.
P320 [Normal] In the case of the Burial Assistance program, the collected data includes the record of the deceased senior citizen, the date of death, claimant’s detail, and the proof of the relationship. The public submission code used today works in such a way that the number of weekdays is counted between date of death and submission and stops its operation if it is over 30. Therefore, holidays are not considered, and the deadline is prolonged for 45 days.
P322 [Normal] Possible Duplicate Records
P323 [Normal] The implemented checking system gives additional points to the following a matching senior citizen ID gets 100 points, a matching name gets 45 points, a matching birth date gets 35 points, a matching contact number gets 25 points, and a matching barangay gives 10 points. If the score builds at least 60 points, the system indicates an individual to be reviewed further for duplication. For example, the matching name and birth date give 80 points, which warns against duplicating. Also, a match of barangay does not give warning as it only gains 10 points.
P325 [Normal] Home Visits and Application Status
P326 [Normal] The general representatives of OSCA organize visits of the authorized personnel. The staff of SHDO computes the results of the in-house visits regarding the information about living conditions, finances, and medical requirements. The review of the determination is done by the general representatives of OSCA. The initiation of the home visit does not ensure the necessity of priority.
P327 [Normal] The applications for public assistance are submitted for the review process at OSCA. Reviewers identify if the changes must be done after which the new submission is checked. The process proceeds with information on decisions made by the OSCA personnel after making a required determination.
P329 [Normal] Historical Records and Reports
P330 [Normal] The structure and value acceptance of historical excel or csv records is verified before their import. The preview stage identifies errors. The source and accuracy of the information is verified by OSCA personnel. The report generates application records by-service, barangay, status and date without changing their previous decisions.

P362 [Normal] Data Analysis Procedures
P363 [Normal] Frequency counts determined how many respondents chose each rating. To determine weighted mean, the frequency of each rating was multiplied by the rating value, the scores obtained were summed and the total value was divided by the valid ratings. Area means were based on all ratings from the given area. Overall mean was based on all 300 ratings instead of unweighted means of three group means.
P364 [Normal] The results were presented rounded to two decimal digits. The approved ranges were from 3.26 to 4.00 for the Strongly Agree rating, from 2.51 to 3.25 for the Agree rating, from 1.76 to 2.50 for the Disagree rating and from 1.00 to 1.75 for the Strongly Disagree rating. The discussion of findings was carried out without applying any significance tests. Accuracy is presented as an item rating and not as the accuracy ratio.

P385 [Normal] The findings from SENIORLINK’s design, creation, evaluation, and testing are discussed in this chapter. The discussion is based on the Statement of the Problem, which states five questions. The first four of the questions are answered by giving a detailed account of the system functionality, the workflow, the controls and rule validation processes and the assistance given to seniors in the online environment or through home visits. P386 [Normal] SENIORLINK combines information regarding the profiles of senior citizens, online applications, requirements uploaded, application history, results of home visit, information regarding benefits, and digital Senior Citizen IDs issued by local agencies. P736 [Normal] SOP 4  Rule-Based Checking Process
P737 [Normal] How can SENIORLINK support accurate and accessible application processing through rule-based validation of incomplete information, invalid entries, missing requirements, and possible duplicate records, while providing online assistance and scheduled home visits for bedridden, ill, or mobility-impaired senior citizens without granting special application priority or replacing the final eligibility decision of authorized OSCA personnel?
P738 [Normal] SENIORLINK ensures accurate and efficient processing of applications through its interconnected workflow. This allows for the combination of basic rule-based validation as well as online application help and arranged visits to patients at home when necessary. A set series of rules are used to find issues, while the staff is held responsible for processing the information provided by a customer.
P739 [Normal] The rule-based process occurs before and during Main OSCA review:
P740 [Normal] Check completeness of information and requirements. The system checks required fields and service-specific electronic documents. Missing information or documentation is flagged so that it can be addressed before or during review.
P741 [Normal] Validate formats and entries. The system checks whether entries follow expected formats, such as dates, identifiers, and accepted file types. Invalid entries are flagged for correction or review.
P742 [Normal] Flag possible duplicate records. The system compares available identifying information with existing records and may flag possible matches. A match is an alert for further checking; the system does not automatically merge or delete records or reject an applicant.
P743 [Normal] Display service-related reminders. Configured rules can display reminders or warnings about required information, documents, or service conditions. The messages help staff identify what needs to be checked but do not establish final eligibility.
P744 [Normal] Provide online application assistance. Senior citizens may complete the public online application themselves or receive assistance from a family member or authorized representative. Assistance does not remove the required documents or change the normal review criteria.
P745 [Normal] Arrange home visits when needed. When an in-person check is required or the applicant has difficulty traveling, Main OSCA determines whether a home visit is needed and assigns it to the appropriate SHDO personnel. The assigned personnel record the findings and submit them through SENIORLINK.
P746 [Normal] Return findings to Main OSCA for review. Main OSCA considers the home-visit findings together with the application, supporting documents, and other relevant information. A home visit does not automatically approve the application or grant priority in the application queue.
P747 [Normal] Retain human decision-making authority. Rule-based alerts and home-visit findings are supporting information only. The authorized Main OSCA personnel make and record the final eligibility decision.
P749 [Normal] Thus, verification makes it possible to distinguish between the records which need alterations or action. It helps ensure stability and spotting, but the last decision regarding eligibility is made by a human and noted by Main OSCA.
P750 [Normal] The same verification procedure can be observed in the historical import presented in Figure 7, the eligibility reminder shown in Figure 12, and legal references shown in Figure 16. These interfaces work hand in hand with Main OSCA by pointing out any records that need validation, confirmation, and further scrutiny. The screens do NOT substitute the proof submitted by the applicant. The authorized agent continues to be responsible for the noted decision.

P753 [Normal] SOP 4 shows that SENIORLINK has combined rules-based validation to facilitate senior citizens in completing their application. The required fields and documents check helps to identify whether there is any missing information, while format checks are done to find entries that do not meet term requirements. Possible duplicate records are to be flagged for review by the staff and not merged or deleted automatically.
P754 [Normal] The use of fixed rules can be linked to Papadopoulos et al. (2022) whose research discussed the expression of know-how as rules and using them to verify the details of a case. Their research relates to the field of clinical decision support, which is nevertheless applied in SENIORLINK. In the latter system, the application of the so-called "static criteria" makes it possible to prepare applications for completion or update. However, these criteria cannot be used independently to determine the eligibility for the benefit.
P755 [Normal] SENIORLINK enables senior citizens to receive assistance in submitting online public forms from their family members or representatives. With local pension applications that involve a home visit, the main OSCA coordinates the visit, and the findings are recorded by SHDO staff. These findings are part of the application information to be reviewed. The assistance and home visits are designed to facilitate the execution of the procedure without providing special queue priority.
P756 [Normal] Differentiation between validation and decision-making connects with works of Enqvist (2024) and Busuioc (2021). The previous content points out that fixed guidelines have certain restrictions, and advanced technologies applied in public administration require an account and the possibilities for human assessment. The completion of the validation process does not ensure the correctness of the input information.
P757 [Normal] Validation and accessibility have different roles in SENIORLINK. Validation identifies information that needs attention, while assisted applications and home visits help applicants through the process. The workflow must clearly separate SHDO field findings from Main OSCA’s administrative decision. Testing of validation rules, home visit submissions, and allowed status changes is required to ensure that the system does its job effectively.

P910 [Heading 1] CHAPTER 4
CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
P911 [Normal] This section discusses the findings of the study “SENIORLINK: A Rule-Based Digital Profiling and Workflow Authentication System for Senior Citizen Services in Pasig City.” The findings are based on the research questions posed in Chapter 1 as well as the system features discussed in Chapter 3.
P912 [Normal] The study focused on designing a web-based application aimed at the management of profiles and applications of senior citizens in Pasig City. SENIORLINK is a total package of online applications, electronic requirements, historical records, application tracking, home visits, benefit information, and temporary digital Senior Citizens ID.
P913 [Normal] The research was conducted using a developmental research design that employed a quantitative descriptive evaluation. The system was produced using the Rapid Application Development method. The evaluation was performed by 20 respondents consisting of 12 Main OSCA personnel, four senior citizens, and four IT experts using a 15-item questionnaire. The survey questionnaire was used to assess the following aspects of the system: Functionality, Reliability, Usability, Efficiency, Portability, and Maintainability using a four-point rating scale.
P918 [Heading 2] Conclusion
P919 [Normal] The following conclusions address the five research questions of the study.
P920 [Normal] 1. Management of Applications and Centralized Records
P921 [Normal] SENIORLINK is a centralized scheme designed for undertaking the administration of senior citizen's profiles along with various applications related to services available to them. Application is available virtually, along with important features such as documents in electronic format for the record as well as importing previous records in Excel format. The benefits are announced through this plan along with the records of home visits and allotted number for issuing temporary ID cards.
P922 [Normal] Connecting the records allows authorized users to check the information and application history of the senior in one platform. Senior citizens and their representatives may apply via the public application, while the Main OSCA staff and authorized personnel will check the information. This facility helps to ensure proper documentary information. However, the establishment of any significant improvements in the speed of processing and accuracy of the records is also essential as they require performance and validation results.
P923 [Normal] 2. Identity Verification and Application Tracking
P924 [Normal] The tracking number used by SENIORLINK is a Permanent Tracking Number. This number is associated with the profile of the senior citizen. After the senior citizen has submitted his/her Senior Citizen ID application, the official ID number is assigned to him/her, and both the tracking number and official ID number can be utilized to fill out benefit applications.
P925 [Normal] Each request submitted is assigned a unique Benefit Tracking number. This allows different applications to keep separate records of their respective details and status while still connected to the same senior citizen. The Permanent Tracking Number provides access to connected applications while identifying a specific request. These numbers do not automatically determine the eligibility status of the applicant or give priority status.
P926 [Normal] 3. Role-Based and Barangay-Based Access
P927 [Normal] SENIORLINK implements several different roles and barangay assignments to enforce restrictions on employees’ access to functions and records in connection with their jobs. Meaning, while main OSCA staff is allowed to access city functions, SHDO employees are allowed only to perform monitoring and activities allowed in barangay office space.
P928 [Normal] There are controls in place that create a basis for the division of duties as well as restriction of access to pertinent information that can be used. In addition to this, such controls should be put to the test with respect to the prohibited actions, which include access to records belonging to other barangays and registration of accounts outside of the user’s allowed functionality. Account management and application activities should be based upon the level of authority granted to each role.
P929 [Normal] 4. Rule-Based Validation and Accessible Application Processing
P930 [Normal] In the checking of information needed, age limits, qualifications, supporting documents, and possibilities for duplicating records, SENIORLINK applies a range of prescribed rules. The checks enable identification of applications that require adjustments or additional examination. While they support the processing chain, the checks do not prove entitlements to benefits on their own.
P931 [Normal] The family member or representative can help the senior participants in filling the application forms through the public portal. It also provides the necessary features for local pension visits scheduling carried out by the Main OSCA and helps the SHDO personnel record the information. Validation and assistance serve different purposes: validation checks the information in the application while assistance and home visits facilitate the process for applicants.
P932 [Normal] It should be ensured that the information obtained while visiting at home is handed over to the chief OSCA and is not subsequently approved automatically or preferentially placed in the queue. There should always be a clear differentiation between the fieldwork undertaken and the final administrative decision that is made.
P933 [Normal] 5. Software Quality Evaluation
P934 [Normal] According to the respondents, SENIORLINK was rated with a total average score of 3.66, which means Strongly Agree. Accordingly, the averages corresponding to the areas of the study are as follows: 3.75 for Functionality, 3.58 for Reliability, 3.68 for Usability, 3.70 for Efficiency, and both 3.60 for Portability and Maintainability, while the average scores for all the areas fall into the Strongly Agree range.
P935 [Normal] From these responses, 300 ratings were produced where there were 198 Strongly Agree scores and 102 Agree scores. The total weighted score was equal to 1098. In terms of the performance of each group, Main OSCA scored at the average of 3.67 while senior citizens scored 3.82 and IT experts scored 3.47.
P936 [Normal] This implies that respondents responded together positively. However, Reliability, Portability, and Maintainability were rated much lower in comparison and thus would need further testing and improvement. Ratings reflect the opinions of selected respondents but do not constitute factual evidence of system performance, safety, or reliability.
P937 [Normal] According to the results of the research, SENIORLINK delivers a platform for linking records for senior citizens and service application systems. The results and conclusions of this study are valid only for the prototype built for this purpose, the presented test and the 20 respondent participants. The staff has not taken part in the evaluation. More testing as well as user evaluation will be required before the solutions can be implemented at the greater scale.
P939 [Heading 2] Recommendations
P940 [Normal] Based on the findings and conclusions, the researchers recommend the following improvements to SENIORLINK and areas for future research.
P942 [Normal] Recommendations for the Improvement of the System or Process.
P943 [Normal] It is essential that SENIORLINK be subjected to further assessments before being introduced on a larger scale. Despite receiving Strongly Agree ratings in all six quality domains, the findings do not imply the absence of any outstanding issues. In particular, the areas of Reliability, Portability, and Maintainability must be given close consideration as they received much lower means than the rest – 3.58, 3.60, and 3.60 correspondingly.
P944 [Normal] Backup and recovery protocols must be evaluated consistently. Authorized individuals must ascertain whether the backup can effectively restore the system or the databases after disruption or loss of data. The copies of the backup must be secured, and there must be clear instructions for the responsible personnel on how to recover the operation of the system.
P945 [Normal] Whenever there are changes in staff responsibilities, it is also important to review access permission. Creating accounts, assigning barangay, and accessing sensitive records must be appropriate for the access role of the user. Testing must also include trying to access records of other barangays and doing actions outside of the permitted limits of the account.
P946 [Normal] The rules for validation used in the system need to be reviewed whenever there is a change in office procedures or official requirements. The checks for age, mandatory fields, document requirements or conditions and duplicate detection rules must be aligned with the requirements of the particular office. The authorized staff at OSCA are expected to keep reviewing alerts and documentation and avoid assuming that the system check can serve as the evidence of eligibility.
P947 [Normal] The Local Pension home-visit process requires clear coordination between the personnel of Main OSCA and SHDO. The main OSCA should conduct the visits while the SHDO personnel should document the observations and pass on the findings to the concerned authorities. The field findings should remain distinct from the final decision. A home visit should not just mean that an application is given approval or treated as a priority in the queue.
P948 [Normal] There should be additional evaluations that should include both SHDO staff and authorized representatives. The investigation relied on the results obtained from 12 Main OSCA representatives as well as four elderly citizens and four IT specialists. Taking into account the feedback from additional user categories would shed light on barangay monitoring, assisted application, and home visit work. Training and users' guidelines should also be developed in case of system application on a wider scale.
P950 [Normal] Suggestions for Future Researchers
P951 [Normal] Future researchers could explore SENIORLINK research using a greater number of datasets, participants, and barangays. There are functional tests and performance tests which would determine the actions of the application in regard to processing, retrieval of records, checking of historical record importing procedure, and behaviors of the system under heavy load conditions.
P952 [Normal] Future research may focus on accessibility issues for older individuals with varying degrees of digital literacy, as well as those who depend on intermediaries. These studies might help to determine whether the shapes, guidelines, tracking features, and assistance procedures are clear and useful to the target users.
P953 [Normal] Exploring integration with other governmental databases could also be an option. However, it should be noted that this will depend on the appropriate approvals, data-requirements, and technical capabilities of the systems involved in this potential integration. Currently, the system does not connect directly to government databases (e.g. names of ventures, eGovPH, banks and digital wallets system). If there is to be any future integration of the systems, it will first need to be justified.
P954 [Normal] In the future, researchers should make a separation between client reports and verified evidence from functional tests and test performance. With future developments, it is essential to maintain the role of SENIORLINK in the process of reviewing applications and leaving all decisions in the hands of authorized administrators.

P1000 [Normal] APPENDIX A : Operational Definition of Terms
P1013 [Normal] Application Status - This term denotes the status of an application through the SENIORLINK flow. The application status shows whether the application is still pending, needs revisions, or is finalized. Applicants use this information to follow their applications as well as authorized persons use it to know what further action is necessary.
P1014 [Normal] Application Tracking number - Refers to the unique number assigned to the application for each particular service. Every application for benefits has a different tracking number since it allows following its requirements, current status, and decision separately. For instance, a senior person applying for two types of benefits will have two application numbers despite the fact that both applications refer to one and the same person.
P1015 [Normal] Authorized Representative -  This means a person who can provide help to a senior citizen in the process of making an application for aid. Such representatives may participate in entering the information into the application, uploading the relevant documents, and tracking the progress of the application within the open access area on the Internet. However, it does not change the level of requirements in the application or give access to the records of the senior citizen.
P1016 [Normal] Barangay-Based Access -  It indicates limits of the use of databases by the personnel of the SHDO according to the assigned barangay. This access allows the personnel of the SHDO to have access to the local records, monitor the progress of applications, and perform the necessary operations. However, such access is limited only to one barangay and does not extend to the other barangays.
P1017 [Normal] Centralized Senior Citizen Profile - Refers to a basic electronic system of record-keeping that unifies senior citizen’s details. It includes vital statistics and corresponding files, documents on application status, and living history. This system allows authorized users to verify relevant details instead of going through individual records for every single inquiry.
P1018 [Normal] Digital Profiling - Refers to the procedure of collecting, arranging, storing, and updating information about a senior citizen in SENIORLINK electronically. The data includes personal information such as name, date of birth, contact number, address, barangay, etc. Digital profiling results in a well-arranged record that can be used in the course of application evaluation and service management.
P1019 [Normal] Electronic Activity Log - Refers to the log maintained by the system with respect to the relevant actions performed in connection with the operations of the system. This diary shows the action undertaken, the user of the system, the record tackled, and the timing of the action. This log will allow authorized people to retrace the prior actions and find change but by itself will not ensure compliance with auditing requirements.
P1020 [Normal] Electronic Requirements - Refer to the electronic copies of the evidence uploaded through SENIORLINK for a particular application. These files are connected to the application and would therefore allow the authorized personnel to check the uploaded files while conducting the application review. Uploading a document does not mean that the information provided is genuine or that the applicant is compliant with all requirements.
P1021 [Normal] Eligibility -  This term refers to the ability of the applicant to qualify for any specific service, which can depend on a wide range of factors relating to age, supportive documentation, and other criteria relevant to the service applied for. The verification messages as well as supportive information are provided by SENIORLINK while being eventually verified by authorized personnel from Main OSCA.
P1022 [Normal] Functionality - This term refers to how exactly SENIORLINK performs its functions. The functionality of SENIORLINK includes managing individual profiles, processing applications, and managing records. When being evaluated, functionality takes into account such factors as suitability, accuracy, and security.
P1023 [Normal] Historical Record Import - This is the same as importing existing data of elderly individuals from Excel or CSV files to SENIORLINK. Before confirming the import, SENIORLINK will evaluate the format of file and the information it contains and show a preview of data being transferred. The relevant authorized personnel of Main OSCA must go through the records for any problems found by SENIORLINK and be responsible for verifying the source information.
P1024 [Normal] Home-Visit Evaluation - This means the results of the investigation of the applicant carried out by the selected SHDO staff members during their visit to him/her. The findings of the investigation may cover the information about the applicant's living conditions, financial situation, health condition, etc. The report is submitted by SHDO to Main OSCA through SENIORLINK with other application materials.
P1025 [Normal] Home-Visit Scheduling - Is the process of organizing and scheduling a visit for the applicant's home through the Main OSCA system. When an applicant is unable to travel, or the treatment requires a visit to the field, a visit is scheduled. The schedule links up the application to those carrying out the visit, but provides neither approval nor any priority to the applicant.
P1026 [Normal] Identity Verification - Involves checking whether the information entered by a user aligns with an approved user record. When applying for some local benefits, the user must input the ID issued by a Senior Citizen ID and the number for permanent tracking. Whenever a match is found, it helps connect the request with the correct user record.
P1027 [Normal] Local Benefit Release Scheduling - Refers to the date and time and place of local benefit release set by official personnel from Main OSCA. The schedule gives the information to personnel to organize the release action. SENIORLINK keeps records and schedule about the mentioned activity; however, it does not pay funds, make banking operations, or deliver any money.
P1028 [Normal] Local Benefits - Denotes the services provided by SENIORLINK in Pasig City, with the following services being provided: enrollment in Land Bank Cash Card, local senior pension, milestone cash gift, and burial assistance. Each service has its own requirements, the necessary information, documentations, and assessment. Knowledge management system supports the process of application and monitoring of the services under study.
P1029 [Normal] Local Digital Senior Citizen ID - This term is used for the electronic display of the Local Senior Citizen ID approved record in the system known as SENIORLINK. The authorized users view identification details associated with the approved record. It differs from the national digital ID found in eGovPH, as the current study does not provide for its use in place of the common physical OSCA card.
P1030 [Normal] Main OSCA - Refers to the official Office for Senior Citizens Affairs based in the city of Pasig. Authorized Main OSCA officials in the SENIORLINK system process applications, review documents, maintain records of senior citizens, make home visits and record final decisions. In addition, they are responsible for uploading historical records of information, processing, reporting, and scheduling local digital ID releases.
P1031 [Normal] Main OSCA Administrator - A designation attributed to the Main OSCA user who receives authorization to complete tasks related to account management and administration. The task consists of creating and managing the staff accounts, as well as assigning roles and barangays on the basis of access rights granted by the system. The administrator may only perform those actions that are permitted through the user account.
P1032 [Normal] Maintainability - A term that includes a measure of how easily the SENIORLINK can be analyzed, fixed, and made to function once there is a need in resolving problems. The evaluation incorporates the aspects related to the analyzability and testability.
P1033 [Normal] Permanent Senior Tracking Number - The Permanent Senior Tracking Number signifies the permanent reference assigned to a Senior citizen's profile when registering. It serves the purpose of monitoring the registration and then entering its details together with the Senior Citizen ID Number into local benefits applications after securing successful approval. Unlike the Tracking Number, this number stays with the Senior citizen through various requests for services.
P1034 [Normal] Portability -  Portability refers to how easily SENIORLINK could be implemented and used in the targeted environment. In the evaluation of portability, it includes installability and replaceability. Installability entails the installation of the software.
P1035 [Normal] Possible Duplicate Record - Denotes a record with details that may match the details in another record of a senior citizen that is already in the records. SENIORLINK compares data like senior citizen ID number, Name, date of birth, contact number, and barangay for possible matches. If a match is flagged, then the staff has to review it because it is possible that the two sets of information are not of the same person.
P1036 [Normal] Public Online Portal - Refers to the portion of SENIORLINK intended for senior citizens and the people supporting them. This will allow people to access the various forms needed for the processing of the requests. They will be submitting their requests via this portal instead of the accounts of the staff members.
P1037 [Normal] Rapid Application Development (RAD) - This term refers to the software development methodology applied in designing the SENIORLINK system through requirements planning, user design, construction, and cutover phases. It allowed for the development of requirements and integrated interfaces in operational modules that could be improved throughout the life-cycle. Thanks to RAD, the research team was able to adjust the system after the completion of tests and identification of organizational needs.
P1038 [Normal] Reliability - It is understood as the potential of the system to keep performing necessary functions and recover when difficulties arise. It deals with coverage of fault tolerance and recoverability. Fault tolerance implies identification of erroneous information and mistakes but without affecting other records. Recoverability, however, refers to the restoration of operation or information after the disruption.
P1039 [Normal] Role-Based Access - This refers to limiting the services a user has access to based on their assigned role and permissions. For example, authorized members of Main OSCA can do app-review tasks while SHDO employees would carry out authorized monitoring and home visits. Thus, this prevents users from taking acts they are not supposed to take.
P1040 [Normal] Rule-Based Validation - This involves verifying information and application conditions with the help of specific if-then statements. This system of SENIORLINK helps in identifying missing fields, incorrect information, non-compliance with submission requests, possible duplicates, and operations contrary to the allowed process.
P1041 [Normal] SENIORLINK - This refers to the online system that was developed as a part of this research to aid senior citizens in the city of Pasig. It links together digital profiles, online applications, electronic documents needed for service, tracking documents, home visits findings, schedules of local benefits as well as authorized digital IDs. The application facilitates the work of the Main OSCA and SHDO staff but maintains the separation of their functions and access rights.
P1042 [Normal] Senior Citizen - This refers to a person aged 60 and older who is part of the demographic referred to in this research project. SENIORLINK provides access to such services as obtaining the local Senior Citizen ID as well as others. However, complying with the general requirement does not necessarily mean that a person will have access to every service as the services require meeting additional criteria.
P1043 [Normal] Senior Help Desk Officer (SHDO) - Refers to personnel who function among local government service workers responsible for senior citizens and for carrying out tasks of local field monitoring and assessment. In SENIORLINK, staff of SHDO are able to obtain necessary info on local government databases and local digital IDs and carry out home visits. The final decision on accepting or rejecting an application lies with someone else (not the SHDO) in the organization or at the level of local authority.
P1044 [Normal] System Efficiency - Indicates the extent to which SENIORLINK can respond to requests for its users according to the usage of its resources available. It deals with two aspects of the system’s functioning in the process of evaluation, namely with time efficiency and resource utilization. Time efficiency means how fast problems were solved and resource utilization depicts how the resources were used during activities such as searching, obtaining and updating.
P1045 [Normal] Usability - Refers to how easily intended users can understand, learn, and operate SENIORLINK. In the evaluation, it covers understandability, learnability, operability, and attractiveness. These criteria concern whether the pages and instructions are clear, the functions are easy to learn and use, and the interface is organized and readable.
P1046 [Normal] Weighted Mean - Refers to the calculation used to summarize respondents’ evaluation ratings. Each rating value is multiplied by the number of respondents who selected it, and the resulting scores are added and divided by the total number of valid ratings. The computed mean is interpreted using the study’s four-point rating scale.
P1047 [Normal] Workflow Authentication - This is the process of identifying users, their entitlements and the operations that they are permitted when using SENIORLINK. This involves checking staff access, verifying the applicant's credentials for a given request as well as controlling permissible activities.
